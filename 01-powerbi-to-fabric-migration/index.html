Playbook 01 · Power BI to Fabric

Migrating from Power BI to Microsoft Fabric

A phase-by-phase guide for moving a Power BI Premium or Pro estate onto Microsoft Fabric. It separates the part that is mandatory and deadline-driven (moving capacity) from the part that creates lasting value (rationalizing, rebuilding and governing what runs on it), and gives you the decisions, templates and checks for both.

  • Audience platform leads, architects, BI and data engineers, program managers
  • Level Intermediate
  • Reading time 120 min

What this playbook covers

Most Power BI estates grew report by report: a PBIX file with its own Power Query logic, its own import copy of the data, and its own DAX. Fabric changes the unit of design. Data lands once in OneLake, is shaped by pipelines, notebooks and a warehouse, and is exposed through a small number of governed semantic models that many reports share. A migration is therefore two different jobs that happen to share a project plan:

  • Move the capacity. Premium per-capacity (P) SKUs are being retired, and the supported path is a Fabric capacity (F SKU). For most tenants this is a licensing and infrastructure change: buy an F SKU, reassign workspaces, validate, cancel the P SKU. Existing reports and semantic models keep working.
  • Modernize the estate. Decide which assets are worth keeping, rebinding or rebuilding on a governed medallion foundation, and retire the rest. This is where the platform benefits come from, and it is optional in the sense that it does not have to finish before the capacity move does.

Many migration guides treat the whole effort as a rebuild. That is the right default for high-value, high-debt assets, but it is the wrong default for a tenant with a licensing deadline. The pages that follow keep the rebuild method intact and put it behind an explicit decision: reassign, modernize in place, or rebuild.

Prerequisites and outcomes

Before you start

  • An executive sponsor and a named business owner per domain.
  • Azure subscription access to register the Microsoft.Fabric resource provider and create capacities.
  • Fabric administrator access, or a service principal allowed to call the read-only admin APIs, for the inventory.
  • The end date of your Premium agreement, confirmed with your Microsoft account team.
  • A Git provider (GitHub or Azure DevOps) and a decision on who owns the repository.

What you end up with

  • Workloads running on an F SKU, with the P SKU cancelled.
  • A signed rationalization matrix and a migration backlog.
  • Governed workspaces, certified semantic models and thin reports.
  • A repeatable CI/CD path from development to production.
  • A support and optimization model owned by named people.

Key takeaways

  • Treat capacity migration and modernization as separate workstreams with separate finish lines; never let the rebuild gate the licensing deadline.
  • Run one inventory and let it feed both workstreams.
  • Decide per asset: reassign, rebind, consolidate, rebuild or retire. Record the decision and get it signed.
  • Put governance and CI/CD in place before the first rebuilt report, not after.
  • Decommissioning is a planned phase with an owner and dates relative to go-live, not a hope.
