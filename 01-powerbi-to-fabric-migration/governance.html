Governance with Fabric and Purview

A single copy of data in OneLake is read by Spark, SQL and the Power BI engine. Controls therefore have to be defined once and applied consistently. This page sets out a govern-first baseline: who decides, what Fabric provides natively, what Microsoft Purview adds, and how trust is earned through endorsement.

  • Phase I · Foundation
  • Audience governance leads, security, Fabric admins, domain owners
  • Output governance policy, tenant-setting baseline, certification criteria

Why govern first

In a Power BI estate each PBIX file was its own island, so governance meant policing sharing. In Fabric the same table is reachable through several engines, workspaces are created by many people, and semantic models fan out to hundreds of reports. Retrofitting controls after teams have built habits means renegotiating with every one of them. A small baseline put in place before the first production workload costs far less than a cleanup project later.

"Govern first" does not mean "govern everything first." It means five things exist before go-live: named owners, a domain structure, a tenant-setting baseline, a label and protection policy, and certification criteria.

What "Purview" means in a Fabric migration now

Fabric has its own built-in governance, centered on the OneLake catalog and its Govern experience: discovery, governance insights, recommended actions, domains, endorsement and lineage. The security insights that used to live in a separate Microsoft Purview hub page inside Fabric are now surfaced in the OneLake catalog. Microsoft Purview remains the layer for information protection (sensitivity labels), data loss prevention, audit, insider risk and AI-usage governance. Plan for both, and do not describe the Fabric experience as the Purview hub.

Step 1: Define objectives and form the governance team

Before configuring any technology, write down what governance must achieve in terms the business recognizes: protect sensitive data, make trusted data easy to find, keep cost visible, and be able to answer "who changed or viewed what." Map those to your actual regulatory and contractual obligations; do not copy a compliance statement from a template.

Step 2: Set the tenant-setting baseline

Tenant settings decide who can create workspaces and Fabric items, share, export, publish externally, use service principals, apply labels and certify content. Decide them as a set, record the reason for each, and delegate to domains or capacities only where you have named owners. The full reference, with recommended values and the reasoning, is in the Tenant Settings Reference.

  • Who may create workspaces, and who may create Fabric items, is a deliberate decision, not a default.
  • Certifiers are designated groups, and certification is delegated per domain only if domain owners are ready.
  • Sharing, export and publish-to-web settings match the label policy.
  • Service principals are allowed to call the APIs your automation needs, scoped to a security group.
  • Label settings (users may apply labels, default labels, mandatory labeling) are decided with the security team.
  • Setting changes are reviewed through the same change process as code.

Step 3: Organize with domains and workspaces

Domains group workspaces by business area and are the basis for federated governance. Create one per business area that has an owner, map every workspace to its domain, and let domain administrators manage their contributors. This is how federated governance is realized in practice. A domain with no owner is a signal to merge it, not to leave it.

Use the workspace naming convention from Architecture so that the domain, layer and environment are visible in every list, lineage view and audit record.

Step 4: Classify and protect with labels and data loss prevention

Define a small label taxonomy with the security team: typically four levels from public to highly confidential. Keep it small enough that people choose correctly. Then decide what each label does, because a label with no consequences is only decoration.

ControlWhat it doesApply it
Sensitivity labelsClassify items and, where configured, encrypt and restrict export. Set on Fabric items. With inheritance enabled, labels flow downstream through lineage, so a labeled lakehouse or model passes its label to derived reports.Set default labels at tenant or workspace level so nothing is unlabeled; require labels on production workspaces.
Sensitive information typesPattern-based detection of personal, financial or health data in content.Use them as DLP conditions rather than asking people to remember to label.
Protection policiesControl access to items based on their label.Reserve for the highest label after testing; they depend on licensing and supported item types.
Data loss preventionPolicies for supported Fabric structured data evaluate labels and sensitive information types, show policy tips, raise alerts and can restrict access.Run in audit-only mode first; move to enforcement when false-positive rates are low.

Labels apply to items, not to tables or columns

It is common to read that you can label individual lakehouse tables or warehouse columns. In Fabric, sensitivity labels are set on items. Column-level classification and discovery of sensitive data inside tables is a data-catalog capability, not a Fabric label. Design your label policy around items, and use warehouse security features to control access to columns.

Verify against Microsoft Learn

Information protection and DLP for Fabric need additional Microsoft Purview or Microsoft 365 licensing, support specific item types, and have specific conditions and actions. Read Information protection in Microsoft Fabric and Configure data loss prevention policies for Fabric and confirm what your licenses and regions support. Sensitivity labels are also not supported by Power BI Desktop projects (PBIP), so apply them in the service.

Step 5: Earn trust with endorsement and certification

Endorsement is how consumers tell the difference between a one-off and the model the finance team stands behind. Use the levels deliberately.

LevelWho can set itMeaning
PromotedAny user with write permission on the item."Ready for others to use." A signal from the owner, not a quality guarantee.
CertifiedOnly designated reviewers; can be delegated to domain administrators."Reviewed against our criteria and authorized as the source for this subject."
Master dataDesignated users; applies to data items such as lakehouses and semantic models."The authoritative source for a core entity such as customers or products." Check its tenant-setting status.

Publish the criteria so authors know what they are working toward. A workable checklist for a certified semantic model:

  • A named owner and a named steward, with a contact channel on the item description.
  • Documented definitions for every measure that business users see, using the naming standard.
  • Data lineage traced to certified or governed sources; no personal-workspace sources.
  • Reconciliation to the legacy report passed and recorded (see Validate and release).
  • Row-level and object-level security tested for each role.
  • A reliable refresh or Direct Lake framing path with alerting on failure.
  • A sensitivity label applied and consistent with its sources.
  • Performance reviewed with the Best Practice Analyzer rules and Performance Analyzer.
  • Reviewed by someone who did not build it.

Step 6: Access control, in the right place

Apply each control at the layer designed for it. A common failure is to rely on a report filter for confidentiality or to give every consumer workspace access to raw layers.

Step 7: Monitor the governance posture

The OneLake catalog's Govern experience gives domain and tenant owners insights into label coverage, endorsement coverage, item curation and recommended actions without leaving Fabric. Purview adds the audit trail and policy reports. Track a short list of measures and review them on a fixed cadence.

Optional: Purview Data Map scanning

If you need one catalog across Fabric and non-Fabric sources, register the Fabric tenant as a source in the Microsoft Purview Data Map and scan it, authorizing the Purview managed identity through a dedicated Microsoft Entra security group. It is not required for the Fabric-native governance in this page. The setup and permissions are in Connect to and manage a Power BI tenant in Microsoft Purview; confirm the steps for Fabric items there.

Key takeaways

  • Put owners, domains, a tenant-setting baseline, a label policy and certification criteria in place before the first production workload.
  • Use the OneLake catalog for Fabric-native governance and Microsoft Purview for labels, DLP, audit and insider risk. They are complementary.
  • Labels apply to items and flow downstream; plan around items, and use warehouse security for column control.
  • Certification needs published criteria and an independent reviewer, or it becomes decoration.
  • Roll out DLP in audit mode first and review a short set of measures on a fixed cadence.
