Playbook 16 · Tenant Settings

Content, models and discovery

Settings that govern how content is created, protected, discovered and distributed: shared semantic models, sensitivity labels, discovery, apps, custom visuals, legacy dataflows and datamarts, and semantic model behavior.

  • Portal groups Workspace, Information protection, Discovery, App, Power BI visuals, Dataflow, Datamart
  • Default stance Enable guardrails, scope creation

Shared semantic models across workspaces

Building reports on a model in another workspace is the backbone of a governed, shared-model approach: one certified model, many reports. Without it every report needs its own copy.

Use semantic models across workspaces

Where
Workspace settings
Default
Not stated
Typical posture
Enable for all — It is the foundation of shared, governed models; the real gate is Build permission.
Scope and delegation
Supports specific security groups and exceptions.
What it controls
Lets users build a report in one workspace on a semantic model in another. Users still need Build permission on the specific semantic model.
Risk or benefit
Without it every report needs its own copy of the model, which multiplies refreshes and definitions. With it, access still flows through Build permission, row-level security and object-level security on the shared model.
Notes
Enable for the organization and steer authors to endorsed models by certifying them. Governance happens at the model, not at this switch.

Combine with Git and deployment pipelines

Information protection and sensitivity labels

Sensitivity labels from Microsoft Purview Information Protection travel with items and exports. The settings below decide whether people can apply labels and whether labels propagate automatically.

What label-based access control does not cover

Enforcement applies inside the tenant where the label was applied, in .pbix files, and in Excel, PowerPoint and PDF files from supported export paths. It does not apply to .csv or .txt exports or across tenants (including OneLake external data shares), and Git export does not include labels. Label settings such as content marking are not applied to content inside Fabric; encryption is applied on supported export paths.

Suggested rollout order

  1. Complete the Purview prerequisites

    Publish labels to users and enable sensitivity labels for Fabric in the Microsoft Purview portal, following the Learn prerequisites.

  2. Enable applying labels for a pilot group

    Use an allow group first and gather feedback on label names and defaults.

  3. Turn on propagation

    Enable downstream inheritance and data-source labels so content is at least as protected as its sources.

  4. Lock down overrides and links

    Disable workspace-admin override and keep the protected-label link guard on.

  5. Widen exports

    Only after labels are in place, widen Excel, PowerPoint and PDF export to more groups.

Discovery

Discovery lets people find content they cannot yet open and request access. They see metadata (name, description, owner) only. That shortens the path to the right data and reduces duplicated work, at the cost of exposing metadata beyond the item's audience.

Make promoted content discoverable

Where
Discovery settings
Default
Not stated
Typical posture
Enable for specific groups — Self-service promotion is a weak trust signal for broad discovery.
Scope and delegation
Supports specific security groups and exceptions.
What it controls
Lets users who can promote content make it discoverable by people who do not have access to it. They see metadata only (name, description, owner) and must request access.
Risk or benefit
Discovery shortens the path to data and reduces duplicate content, but item names, descriptions and owners become visible beyond the item's audience. Promotion is self-service, so quality varies.
Notes
Prefer making certified content discoverable and keep promoted discovery to trusted groups. Write metadata standards so titles and descriptions never contain sensitive values, and exclude restricted groups.
SettingDefaultTypical postureWhat it controls; risk or benefitNotes
Make certified content discoverableNot statedEnable for specific groupsLets certifiers make the content they certify discoverable by users without access.Pairs with a certification standard; a better discovery signal than promotion.
Discover contentNot statedEnable for allLets users find and request access to content that its owners made discoverable.No effect on content that is not discoverable.
Users can find objects in searchNot statedEnable for allIncludes objects such as tables and columns in OneLake catalog and global search. Users find only objects inside items they can access; turn off to limit discovery to top-level items.Low risk because it respects access.

Write metadata for a wider audience

Once content is discoverable, its title, description and owner are visible to people without access. Publish a naming standard and keep sensitive values out of item names and descriptions.

Apps and template apps

Apps distribute curated content to consumers. Two feature families use the word "app", which is a frequent source of misconfiguration.

FeaturePurposeSettings
Workspace apps and organizational appsDistribute reports and models inside the organization.Push apps to end users, Publish apps to the entire organization, Users can discover and create org apps
Template appsPackaged solutions built from a data source, shared with other organizations.Create template organizational apps, Publish template apps, Install template apps, Install template apps not listed in AppSource

Two easy mix-ups

Publish apps to the entire organization is the real setting name; some documents call it pushing apps to the organization, and it is separate from Push apps to end users. And Create template organizational apps is about template apps, not about internal organizational apps. For the end-to-end approach to organizational apps, see Organizational Apps.

Create template organizational apps

Where
App settings
Default
Not stated
Typical posture
Disable — Needed by solution builders only.
Scope and delegation
Group scoping is not stated in the reference; check the setting panel.
What it controls
Lets users create template apps that use semantic models built on one data source in Power BI Desktop. Template apps are meant for distribution as a packaged solution, not for everyday sharing inside the organization.
Risk or benefit
Packaged apps spread a solution to other workspaces and, with the template-app publishing settings, to other organizations. Few enterprises need it.
Notes
Keep it off unless a team publishes reusable solution templates. Internal distribution is normally done with organizational apps; see the organizational-apps playbook.

What it controls
Lets app publishers install an app automatically for selected end users or groups, with no AppSource installation step by the recipient.

Custom visuals

Custom visuals are JavaScript that runs inside reports. The tenant settings control the service; Power BI Desktop needs matching group policy settings, and the Organizational visuals store bypasses the tenant switches by design.

Add and use certified visuals only (block uncertified)

Where
Power BI visuals
Default
Off
Typical posture
Enable for all — Certification is the cheapest available control over visual code.
Scope and delegation
Applies to the entire organization only; groups cannot be selected.
What it controls
When on, only certified Power BI visuals render in reports and dashboards; uncertified visuals from AppSource or .pbiviz files show an error. Visuals added to the Organizational visuals page are not affected, whether certified or not.
Risk or benefit
Custom visuals run JavaScript in the report. Certified visuals are tested not to call external services, so the setting reduces both a data-exfiltration path and rendering and performance surprises.
Notes
Org-wide only. The tenant setting affects the service; use the Power BI Desktop group policy EnableUncertifiedVisuals to align Desktop. Audit existing reports for uncertified visuals before enabling and route needed visuals through an internal review into the Organizational visuals page.

Align Power BI Desktop with group policy

The tenant settings for visuals apply to the Power BI service. For Power BI Desktop, administrators set matching registry values through group policy under Software\Policies\Microsoft\Power BI Desktop\. Each value is 0 to disable and 1 to enable, and Learn documents 1 as the default.

Registry valueAligns with tenant setting
EnableCustomVisualsAllow visuals created using the Power BI SDK
EnableUncertifiedVisualsAdd and use certified visuals only (block uncertified)
AllowCVToExportDataToFileAllow downloads from custom visuals. When the value is 1, a visual can export only if the tenant setting is on and the user is signed in.

Verify against Microsoft Learn

Check the value names, data types and meanings before you deploy a policy: Manage Power BI visuals admin settings. Test with a pilot device group first.

Organizational visuals store

Adding a visual to the Organizational visuals page makes it available whether or not it is certified and regardless of the SDK and certified-only switches. Organizational visuals deploy to Power BI Desktop automatically. Use the store as the controlled release channel: review the visual for data flow and licensing, add it, and tell authors. Teams building report standards can find guidance in Power BI Report Development Standards.

Store behaviors to plan for

  • AppSource visuals in the store update automatically, so a reviewed version can change later. Re-review on your cadence.
  • Deleting a visual from the store is irreversible and stops it rendering in existing reports at once. Disabling it instead keeps it in the list.
  • Power BI Report Server does not support organizational visuals.

Legacy objects: Gen1 dataflows and datamarts

Two features have modern replacements. Both should be retired on a plan, because live content depends on them.

Create and use Gen1 dataflows

Where
Gen1 dataflow settings
Default
Not stated
Typical posture
Disable — Retire legacy dataflows on a plan, not by surprise.
Scope and delegation
Applies to the entire organization only; groups cannot be selected.
What it controls
Lets users create and use Gen1 dataflows, the original Power BI self-service ETL (Power Query Online with storage managed by Power BI). The setting applies to the entire organization and cannot be limited to groups.
Risk or benefit
Gen1 dataflows are the original self-service ETL path, outside the OneLake-based items and CI/CD patterns that Dataflow Gen2 provides. Existing datasets and refreshes may depend on dataflows people already built, so retirement needs a plan.
Notes
Sequence it: inventory Gen1 dataflows with the admin APIs, communicate that new work uses Dataflow Gen2, migrate, then disable. Disabling too early breaks refreshes.

Key takeaways

  • Enable cross-workspace model use for everyone; the real gates are Build permission and row-level security.
  • Deploy labels with inheritance and link restrictions first, then widen exports; remember labels are not enforced on CSV, across tenants or in Git.
  • Prefer certified discovery to promoted discovery, and keep sensitive values out of item metadata.
  • Separate organizational apps from template apps, and use the real setting names: Publish apps to the entire organization and Push apps to end users.
  • Enforce certified visuals only, run an Organizational visuals review route, and align Desktop with group policy.
  • Retire Gen1 dataflows and datamarts on a plan, not by toggling the switch first.
