Playbook 11 · Organizational Apps

Prerequisites and governance

Creating and distributing an org app depends on five layers, from the tenant down to the permissions on each included item. A gap in any layer fails late and confusingly, usually when someone tries to save or share. This page names each layer, who owns it, and how to prove it works before you build.

The five layers

Control narrows as you go down: a Fabric administrator opens the platform-wide gates, a capacity administrator provides the capacity, a workspace administrator decides who builds, and the people who own the items decide who can reshare them. That layering supports delegated administration. Central IT sets policy; domain teams create and run their own apps inside it.

Verify against Microsoft Learn

Two things to confirm in your tenant. First, the exact name and location of the org app setting and of the Real-Time Dashboard creation setting: the Get started article lists workspace type and role as prerequisites and does not name a tenant setting, while the Fabric tenant settings reference documents the org app setting. Second, how to reach tenant settings: Learn describes the Admin portal and the Govern area of the OneLake catalog. Cross-check against the Tenant Settings Reference playbook and the tenant settings index.

Scope settings with groups

Enable the org app setting for a named Entra security group such as sg-fabric-orgapp-creators, start with a pilot team, and add groups as the operating model matures. A tenant-wide switch is rarely the right starting point for a feature that creates distribution paths to data.

Capacity and workspace type

Org apps live in workspaces, and the workspace has to be one of four types: Pro, Fabric trial, Premium capacity or Fabric capacity. Learn calls this setting the workspace type; older material calls it the license mode. You change it in Workspace settings, on the License info tab or the Premium tab depending on your tenant, with Edit.

Workspace typePower BI itemsNon-Power BI Fabric itemsNotes
ProYesNoReports and semantic models only; no notebooks, Real-Time Dashboards or Maps in this workspace
Fabric trialYesYesA 60-day trial with F64-equivalent capacity, suitable for evaluation and proof of concept
Premium capacity (P)YesIf Fabric is enabledLearn notes that P SKUs are being retired in favor of F SKUs; plan new purchases around F
Fabric capacity (F)YesYesF64 or larger allows free-license users with a viewer role to view Power BI content
Premium Per User (PPU)YesNoNot in the list of workspace types that Get started with org apps accepts; do not assume it works

The practical consequence is easy to miss. Org apps work in Pro workspaces, which broadens who can use them, but a Pro workspace cannot hold a notebook or a Real-Time Dashboard. If you want those item types in the app, the workspace must be on a Fabric or trial capacity.

When your tenant has Fabric trials turned on and the workspace is not on a capacity, a user who creates an org app is prompted to start a trial. Decide in advance whether that is acceptable, because a trial workspace is not a production home for a business-critical app.

Per-user licenses

Licensing has two sides. Creators need a license that lets them manage Power BI items. Consumers need a license that depends on the capacity behind the workspace.

Verify against Microsoft Learn

The free-license rule in the licensing article is phrased around a workspace viewer role. Consumers of an org app normally have no workspace role at all; they have access to the app item. The org app article does not restate a consumer license rule. Before you promise broad free-license consumption, run the smoke test below with a free-licensed account that holds no workspace role.

Workspace roles and the write-and-reshare rule

Even with the right tenant setting, capacity and license, what a person can do in a given workspace depends on their role. Users with the Admin, Member or Contributor role can create org app items; a Viewer cannot create items.

Creating the item is the easy part. Anything that changes who has access is stricter. When you add or remove an item, change audience visibility or otherwise affect access management, you need write and reshare permissions on the org app item, on every included item, and on the underlying semantic models that the app grants or revokes access to, in the same workspace or another one. In practice only workspace Admins and Members hold those permissions on everything.

RoleCreate an org app itemEdit without changing accessAdd or remove items, change audiences or access
AdminYesYesYes, typically
MemberYesYesYes, typically
ContributorYesYesOnly with write and reshare on the items and models
ViewerNoNoNo

It is easy to assume that Contributors cannot create apps. They can; what they cannot normally do is change access. The workspace setting Allow contributors to update the app, under the Power BI general settings for organization apps, applies to workspace apps only and has no effect on org apps.

Use this to design roles on purpose. Put report developers in Contributor so they can build and edit content without being able to widen access. Put the small group of people who publish and manage apps in Member, and keep Admin for platform owners. If a Contributor must manage access, grant the specific write and reshare rights on the items instead of promoting them.

Where the app lives

All included items must be in the app's workspace. With the house naming convention that usually means the consumption workspace for a domain, for example PRD_SALES_CNSM, which holds the reports and notebooks, while the semantic models behind the reports can sit in a gold-layer workspace and still receive access through the app, provided the app's author has the rights described above.

  • The org app setting is enabled for a named group that contains the planned creators.
  • Every workspace that will host apps is on a supported workspace type and, if it will hold notebooks or Real-Time Dashboards, on a Fabric or trial capacity.
  • Creators hold Pro or PPU and a Member or Admin role (or specific write and reshare rights).
  • Entra security groups exist for each planned audience, and an owner is named for each group.
  • You have test accounts: one free-licensed with no workspace role, one with a Pro license, and one that belongs to two audiences.
  • You know whether consumers will be on a capacity of F64 or larger and have validated free-license viewing with a test account.
  • The semantic models behind planned reports have identified owners who can grant or confirm reshare rights.

Smoke test before you invest

An hour spent on a throwaway app is cheaper than discovering a gap while stakeholders wait. Use a test workspace that matches the production workspace type.

  1. Create a minimal app

    As a Member, create an org app with one report whose semantic model lives in a different workspace. Add a notebook or Real-Time Dashboard if your design needs them.

  2. Add one audience member

    Share the app with a free-licensed test account that has no workspace role.

  3. Sign in as the test account

    Open the app from the Apps page and confirm that the report renders, the cross-workspace model resolves, and the notebook or dashboard opens.

  4. Check the negative case

    Remove the test account from the app and confirm access to the report and the model is gone, unless it came from another route.

  5. Repeat as a Contributor

    Try to add an item as a Contributor without extra rights and note the error. Your support team will see it.

Key takeaways

  • Verify five layers: tenant settings, capacity, workspace type, role, and item and model permissions.
  • Creators need Pro or PPU in every case; consumer licensing is decided by the capacity behind the workspace.
  • Notebooks and Real-Time Dashboards require a Fabric or trial capacity workspace even though org apps work in Pro workspaces.
  • Contributors can create apps; changing access needs write and reshare on items and models, which in practice means Member or Admin.
  • Validate with real test accounts before promising free-license consumption.
