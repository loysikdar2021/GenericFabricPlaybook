Organizational apps in Microsoft Fabric

An org app packages reports, notebooks, real-time dashboards and other Fabric items into a branded, audience-aware experience for people who never open the workspace. This playbook covers what you need before you start, how to build and govern an app, how access flows from the app to the items beneath it, and how to promote it through environments.

  • Audience BI developers, Fabric and Power BI admins, analytics architects
  • Level Intermediate
  • Reading time 65 min

What an org app is

An org app is a Fabric item type that content creators use to package content for consumers in their organization. You create it inside a workspace (New > Org app), pick items from that same workspace, arrange them into a navigation structure, brand it and share it. The people who open the app do not need a role in the workspace: access to the app item grants them what they need on the included items and on the semantic models behind the reports.

Three properties separate an org app from the classic workspace app (the experience formerly called a Power BI app):

  • Many apps per workspace. A workspace can host as many org apps as you need, each with its own navigation, branding and audiences. A workspace app is limited to one per workspace.
  • Live references, not versioned copies. Included items stay where they are. When you save the app, the change is visible to viewers immediately, and edits to an included item are visible as soon as that item is saved. There is no publish or update step for the app.
  • Access is item access. Adding a person to an app grants Read on the included items and on the underlying semantic models; removing the person revokes the access that the app granted. Consumers find the app like any other item, with no install step.

Org apps do not replace workspace apps. Both coexist, and existing workspace apps are not renamed, upgraded or migrated automatically. If you want org app features for existing content, you build a new org app (see the migration steps).

The org app item is documented as generally available, but the pieces around it move at different speeds: inclusion of Fabric Apps is a preview feature, and the Git and deployment-pipeline entries for org apps carry a preview label in the supported-items lists. The trade-off that comes with live references is governance. Because nothing is staged inside the app, a saved change to a report is immediately in front of every consumer. That is why the playbook treats separate development, test and production workspaces as part of the design, not an optional extra (see ALM).

When to use an org app, and when not to

Use an org app when

  • You are curating content for people outside the workspace, inside your tenant
  • One set of items must serve several audiences with different views
  • You want notebooks, real-time dashboards or maps next to reports
  • Reports depend on semantic models in other workspaces and you want access handled for you
  • You want Git and pipeline-based promotion for the app itself

Prefer something else when

  • The consumers are external customers or an application (embedding)
  • You need classic dashboards or scorecards (workspace app)
  • You depend on a staged publish-and-update step inside one workspace (workspace app)
  • You distribute a packaged solution to many tenants (template app)
  • You need custom front-end logic (a Fabric App, which you can then include)

The full decision table, with licensing and access consequences, is on Concepts and choosing an app model.

Prerequisites at a glance

  • Tenant. The org app tenant setting is on for the creators, and "Users can create Fabric items" is on if you include non-Power BI items.
  • Workspace type. The workspace is a Pro, Fabric trial, Premium capacity or Fabric capacity workspace; notebooks and Real-Time Dashboards need a Fabric or trial capacity workspace.
  • Creator license. Power BI Pro or Premium Per User, because org apps are Power BI items.
  • Role. Contributor, Member or Admin to create; write and reshare on the included items and models to change access.
  • Consumer license. Depends on the capacity size; free-license viewing is tied to F64 or larger, P or trial capacity.

Details, owners and a pre-flight checklist are on Prerequisites and governance.

What you will be able to do

  • Choose between an org app, a workspace app, direct sharing, embedding and a template app, and justify the choice.
  • Configure the tenant, capacity, license and role layers so creation and distribution do not fail late.
  • Design an app around its audience, then build, brand and test it.
  • Include each supported item type and know exactly where access is, and is not, handled for you.
  • Share through audiences and groups, revoke cleanly, and explain why a removed user may still see something.
  • Version the app in Git, promote it through development, test and production, and retire it safely.

Key takeaways

  • An org app is a Fabric item that references items in its workspace; saving the app or an included item is immediately visible to consumers.
  • Use several small, audience-specific apps from one workspace instead of one app for everyone.
  • Access follows the app: adding or removing a person grants or revokes Read on included items and their semantic models, with documented exceptions.
  • Org apps coexist with workspace apps; there is no automatic migration, and some workspace app features have no org app equivalent.
  • Treat status labels, tenant setting names and CI/CD support as facts to re-check, not facts to remember.
